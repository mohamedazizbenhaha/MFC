# Laboratory Manual: Practical Construction of a Submersible Tubular Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC)

**Course:** Practical Bioelectrochemical Systems
**Level:** Introductory / Hands-on Workshop
**Duration:** 1 Session (3-4 hours)
**Instructor:** Practical Expert
**Primary Resources:** Final Technical Diagram [Attached], Definitive Technical Dossier [For Reference]

---

## 1. Introduction: Building a Bio-Battery
Welcome to this practical session on constructing a Microbial Fuel Cell (MFC)! Today, you will learn how to assemble a functional, submersible tubular MFC from individual components. This device uses bacteria to generate electricity from organic matter, acting like a small, bio-powered battery. Our focus will be on the precise mechanical assembly and wiring to ensure your MFC is ready for operation.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives
By the end of this practical session, you will be able to:
1.  Identify and prepare all components required for a tubular MFC.
2.  Assemble the MFC structure, including the unique "chimney" and cathode integration.
3.  Correctly wire the anode and cathode to an external circuit.
4.  Perform a basic integrity test to ensure the MFC is ready for deployment.

## 3. Materials and Equipment

### 3.1 Provided Components
*   1x Inner Cylinder (Acrylic, 10 cm L x 2 cm OD, male threads)
*   1x Outer Cylinder (Acrylic, 17 cm L x 3 cm OD, upper 3 cm solid/threaded, lower 14 cm perforated)
*   1x Stainless Steel Mesh (316L, 2.8 cm diameter)

*   1x External Resistor (e.g., 1000 $\Omega$)
*   2x Insulated Wires (approx. 30 cm each, pre-stripped ends)
*   1x Small black connector piece (for carbon blade eyelet)

## 5. Step-by-Step Construction Guide
Follow these instructions carefully to assemble your tubular MFC. Refer to the attached technical diagram for visual guidance.

### 5.1 Step 1: Preparing the Cathode Assembly
1.  Take one **Silicone Gasket** and place it flat on your workbench.
2.  Carefully place the **Commercial Air-breathing Cathode Disk** on top of the gasket.
3.  Place the second **Silicone Gasket** on top of the cathode disk, creating a "sandwich" (Gasket - Cathode - Gasket).
4.  Take the **Stainless Steel Mesh** and place it on top of this sandwich. This mesh will serve as the current collector for the cathode.

### 5.2 Step 2: Integrating the Cathode into the Outer Cylinder
1.  Take the **Outer Cylinder** (the longer, wider cylinder with perforations at the bottom and threads at the top).
2.  Carefully insert the entire cathode assembly (gaskets, cathode disk, mesh) into the top, threaded section of the Outer Cylinder. Ensure it sits snugly at the bottom of the threaded section.
    *   **Important:** The mesh should be facing upwards, ready to be in contact with the inner cylinder.

### 5.3 Step 3: Connecting the Inner Cylinder (The "Chimney")
1.  Take the **Inner Cylinder** (the shorter, narrower cylinder with male threads at the bottom).
2.  Carefully screw the Inner Cylinder into the top, threaded section of the Outer Cylinder. As you screw it down, it will press against the Stainless Steel Mesh, securing the entire cathode assembly in place.
    *   **Caution:** Do not overtighten. Ensure it is firm but not stressed. The top of the Inner Cylinder should remain open.
    *   **Result:** You have now created the sealed "chimney" for air supply to the cathode and secured the cathode assembly.

### 5.4 Step 4: Preparing and Inserting the Anode (Carbon Blade)
1.  Take the **Pre-fabricated Iron-doped Biochar Blade**. Notice the eyelet (small loop with a hole) at the top of the blade.
2.  Attach one of the **Insulated Wires** to this eyelet. If a small black connector piece is provided, use it to secure the wire to the eyelet.
3.  Carefully slide the Carbon Blade into the hollow part of the Outer Cylinder, ensuring it hangs centrally.
    *   **Critical Positioning:** The top of the Carbon Blade (where the wire is attached) should be positioned approximately **2 cm below the Stainless Steel Mesh**. This prevents any direct contact or short-circuiting between the anode and cathode.
4.  Route the wire connected to the Carbon Blade out of the MFC. It should pass through one of the $\varnothing$ 3 mm holes in the perforated section of the Outer Cylinder wall.

### 5.5 Step 5: External Wiring
1.  Take the second **Insulated Wire** and connect it directly to the **Stainless Steel Mesh**. This wire will carry electrons from the cathode.
2.  Connect the wire coming from the **Carbon Blade** and the wire coming from the **Stainless Steel Mesh** to the two terminals of the **External Resistor** (e.g., 1000 $\Omega$).
    *   **Result:** Your MFC is now a complete electrical circuit, ready to generate power once bacteria are introduced.

## 6. Integrity Test (Mandatory)
Before introducing any biological components, it is crucial to test the mechanical integrity of your assembled MFC.

1.  **Submersion Test:** Carefully submerge the entire assembled MFC (excluding the top opening of the Inner Cylinder) into a beaker filled with water.
2.  **Observation:** Observe for any air bubbles escaping from the sealed junctions (especially around the cathode assembly) or water ingress into the inner chimney cylinder.
3.  **Troubleshooting:** If leaks are observed, carefully disassemble the relevant section, re-seat the gaskets, and re-tighten the threads. Repeat the test until no leaks are detected.
